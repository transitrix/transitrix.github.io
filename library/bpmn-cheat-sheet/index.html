Pools and lanes

Pool: participant, possibly a black box.
Lane: grouping within a process.

Read the flow before the detail

Identify the participants, follow sequence arrows within each pool, then read the gateway markers and event triggers. A message flow describes communication, not a transfer of process control.

The event examples distinguish start, intermediate and end positions, then catching and throwing messages and a catching timer. An empty End Event completes that path; it does not cancel other active paths. The boundary examples use timers attached to activities.

The Event-Based Gateway shown is the ordinary exclusive variant: the first configured event to occur determines the path. Its successors are supported catching intermediate events or Receive Tasks, subject to the specification's restrictions. Instantiating variants are outside this sheet.

This is a selection for reading diagrams, not a complete catalogue of BPMN events, activity types or gateways, and not an execution or conformance guide.

Source and scope

OMG Business Process Model and Notation, version 2.0.2, formal/2013-12-09. The normative specification defines the symbols and their detailed connection and execution rules.

Relevant clauses: 7.3 (notation overview), 7.6 (connections), 9.3-9.4 (participants and messages), 10.3 (activities and loop characteristics), 10.5 (events), 10.6 (gateways) and 10.8 (lanes). The multi-instance markers follow 10.3.8 and Figures 10.48-10.49: vertical for parallel, horizontal for sequential.
